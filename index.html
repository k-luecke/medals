Current work

Time integrity from shared geophysical signals

Manuscript in preparation

Networked clock synchronization is exposed to delay manipulation that cryptography alone cannot detect. We study hardening synchronization by anchoring time to naturally occurring signals that every node can measure independently — so a timing claim can be verified against physics rather than trusted to the network.

Information-theoretic authentication

Open problem under study

Biometric key generation and multi-source fusion, evaluated against formal privacy-leakage bounds rather than heuristic security arguments. Current focus is a characterization problem we consider genuinely open; the work is paused, deliberately, until it is resolved.

Research methodology

In continuous use

A structured process for locating genuinely open problems: each is framed with demonstrable constraints, a hard floor traced to a theorem or physical bound, and a preregistered confirm / refute / inconclusive decision rule fixed before any measurement is taken.

How we work
Preregister before measuring

Decision rules are committed and hashed before experiments run, so outcomes can't be negotiated afterward.

Negative results are results

A refutation under a preregistered rule is a finding, and it gets reported as one.

Claims scale with evidence

Scope is stated plainly. If a limitation exists, it appears in the paper, not in the fine print.

Verify independently

Data, scripts, and deposits are published so any result can be reproduced without asking permission.
